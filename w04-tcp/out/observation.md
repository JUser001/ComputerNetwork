## Task 1 — Reliability
I chose stop-and-wait: one outstanding segment makes retransmission and ACK validation simple, while `expected_seq` prevents duplicate data from being appended and stale ACKs are ignored. With seed 246, 2,000 bytes arrived byte-identically; the data channel sent 323 packets versus a minimum of 250 (1.292×), with 40 losses and 8 duplicate events, so loss-driven retransmissions were the main reliability cost.

## Task 2 — Measurement and handshake (fill after running on your computer)
**Actual measurements and capture must be collected locally.** For the capture, record the packet numbers for SYN / SYN-ACK / final ACK and both raw initial sequence numbers from the trace; ISNs are chosen per connection rather than starting at zero to avoid ambiguity between separate connection incarnations. Read MSS, window-scale shift count, and SACK-permitted from the SYN options; compute effective receive window as `advertised_window_field × 2^shift_count`, then compare it with peak bytes in flight.

**Time-limited method:** use the same network, label the first five-run sample `Wi-Fi_now`, then label another five-run sample `Wi-Fi_10min_later` about 10 minutes later. This is not two networks and does not fully meet the lab's preference for two very different times of day; disclose that limitation. After running both commands, copy the actual values from `out/throughput.json` here: `Wi-Fi_now` median **___ Mbps**, min–max spread **___ Mbps (___%)**, median TCP handshake **___ ms**; `Wi-Fi_10min_later` median **___ Mbps**, spread **___ Mbps (___%)**, median handshake **___ ms**. Do not fill these blanks with estimates. A longer handshake adds RTT/startup cost before useful transfer; a longer RTT also slows ACK-clocked congestion-window growth. Spread can come from Wi-Fi contention, signal quality, cross-traffic, server/CDN load, and transient congestion.

## Task 3 — Congestion control
The controller's window is capped at 24 packets: the link bandwidth-delay product is `1 packet/slot × 20 slots = 20 packets`, so four extra packets provide modest queue headroom. The benchmark gives 969.8 goodput/1,000 slots (98.3% of baseline), 0.0% loss, and average queue 3.7, meeting the strong threshold; the fixed-window baseline gets slightly more goodput but loses 37.4% and holds an average queue of 8.8, harming other flows. In a tuning experiment with a 32-packet cap, gentler backoff (0.95 instead of 0.70) lifted goodput from 947.2 to 969.8 but raised average queue from 4.24 to 8.07; the final cap of 24 avoids that queueing cost.
